# BIBLIOGRAPHY.md

This bibliography is a curated starting point, not a reading queue. References should be added when they become relevant to the current roadmap stage.

## Foundations — reinforcement learning

### Sutton, R. S. & Barto, A. G. — *Reinforcement Learning: An Introduction*, 2nd ed. (2018)

Primary conceptual backbone for MDPs, value functions, dynamic programming, Monte Carlo, temporal-difference learning, control, function approximation, and policy-gradient methods.

**Planned use:** Stages 1–2.  
**Status:** TO STUDY SYSTEMATICALLY.

## Foundations — multi-agent systems and game theory

### Shoham, Y. & Leyton-Brown, K. — *Multiagent Systems: Algorithmic, Game-Theoretic, and Logical Foundations* (2009)

Reference for game-theoretic and algorithmic foundations of multi-agent systems.

**Planned use:** Stages 0B–4.  
**Status:** REFERENCE.

### Axelrod, R. & Hamilton, W. D. — “The Evolution of Cooperation” (Science, 1981)

Classic connection between repeated Prisoner’s Dilemma, strategy interaction, and evolutionary dynamics.

**Planned use:** Stage 0B.  
**Status:** TO READ.

## Self-play and search

### Silver, D. et al. — “Mastering the game of Go with deep neural networks and tree search” (Nature, 2016)

Important historical reference combining learned policy/value functions with Monte Carlo Tree Search.

**Planned use:** Stages 2 and 5.  
**Status:** LATER.

### Silver, D. et al. — “Mastering the game of Go without human knowledge” (Nature, 2017)

Important reference for self-play learning without human game records as the central training signal.

**Planned use:** Stages 2 and 5.  
**Status:** LATER.

## Multi-agent learning

### Vinyals, O. et al. — “Grandmaster level in StarCraft II using multi-agent reinforcement learning” (Nature, 2019)

Large-scale example of league-based multi-agent training and strategic diversity.

**Planned use:** Stage 4.  
**Status:** LATER.

## Automated discovery

### Fawzi, A. et al. — “Discovering faster matrix multiplication algorithms with reinforcement learning” (Nature, 2022)

AlphaTensor: example of converting an algorithmic/mathematical search problem into an RL-style game.

**Planned use:** Stage 6.  
**Status:** LATER.

### Mankowitz, D. J. et al. — “Faster sorting algorithms discovered using deep reinforcement learning” (Nature, 2023)

AlphaDev: example of discovering low-level algorithmic improvements through reinforcement-learning-based search.

**Planned use:** Stage 6.  
**Status:** LATER.

### Romera-Paredes, B. et al. — “Mathematical discoveries from program search with large language models” (Nature, 2024)

FunSearch: important example of using an LLM as a program-generation component inside an evolutionary/search-and-evaluation loop.

**Planned use:** Stage 7.  
**Status:** LATER.

## Reading policy

Do not attempt to read this list front-to-back.

For each source used:

1. identify the specific question it helps answer;
2. record the chapters/sections actually studied;
3. distinguish what Jonathan understands from what has merely been read;
4. connect useful concepts to `CONCEPT_MAP.md`;
5. add unresolved points to `OPEN_QUESTIONS.md`.

A longer bibliography is not evidence of better understanding.
